This study presents a comprehensive analysis of the SARS-CoV-2 mutational landscape across all major viral proteins and characterizes how the virus interacts with host cellular components — including RNA-binding proteins (RBPs), microRNAs, and the broader human interactome. As the pandemic drove rapid viral evolution, understanding which mutations were accumulating, where they were occurring across the genome, and how they affected host-pathogen interaction networks was critical for informing therapeutic and vaccine development strategies.

## Motivation

The rapid emergence of SARS-CoV-2 variants with altered transmissibility, immune evasion, and clinical severity raised urgent questions about the mutational dynamics of the virus. Beyond cataloguing mutations, understanding how an evolving viral proteome interfaces with host cellular machinery — particularly RNA regulatory networks — was essential for identifying stable therapeutic targets and predicting the functional consequences of ongoing viral evolution.

## What We Built

- Performed a **genome-wide mutational analysis** of SARS-CoV-2 sequences deposited in GISAID, characterizing substitution patterns across structural and non-structural protein-coding regions
- Mapped mutations onto the **SARS-CoV-2 proteome** to identify hotspot regions under positive selection or functional constraint, with particular attention to the spike protein, nucleocapsid, and RNA-dependent RNA polymerase
- Characterized the **host-virus interactome** by analyzing interactions between viral proteins and human host factors, including RNA-binding proteins and microRNA regulatory networks
- Integrated mutational data with interaction network analysis to assess how emerging mutations affect the stability and specificity of host-virus protein-protein and protein-RNA contacts
- Built resources to support real-time tracking of mutation-interaction relationships as new variants emerged

## Key Findings

- Mutations were non-uniformly distributed across the SARS-CoV-2 genome, with structural proteins showing both highly conserved regions and mutation hotspots relevant to immune recognition
- Host RBP interaction sites were enriched at functionally important regions of the viral genome, suggesting post-transcriptional regulation as a key axis of host-pathogen conflict
- The interactome analysis revealed a set of host factors with stable interaction interfaces across variant lineages, representing candidate targets for broadly active therapeutics
