This study evaluates the antiangiogenic potential of *Clerodendrum infortunatum* Linn. (hill glory bower), a plant with a long history in Indian folk medicine, using chorioallantoic membrane (CAM) image analysis as an in vivo model system. Angiogenesis — the formation of new blood vessels — is a hallmark of tumor progression and metastasis, making its inhibition a promising therapeutic target in oncology.

## Background

*Clerodendrum infortunatum* is a flowering shrub native to tropical Asia, traditionally used in Siddha and Ayurvedic medicine to treat a range of conditions including tumors, fever, and skin diseases. Prior work has characterized its phytochemical profile and documented cytotoxic activity of root extracts across multiple human cancer cell lines, with betulinic acid identified as the key active principle. This study extends that work to systematically evaluate antiangiogenic activity.

## Approach

- Applied the **chorioallantoic membrane (CAM) assay** — a well-established in vivo model for quantifying angiogenic response using fertilized chicken eggs
- Developed a **computational image analysis pipeline** to quantify vascular density and network morphology from CAM photographs, enabling objective, reproducible scoring of antiangiogenic activity
- Integrated image-derived vascular features with phytochemical characterization of *C. infortunatum* extracts

## Significance

The CAM assay bridges the gap between in vitro cytotoxicity studies and clinical antiangiogenic endpoints. By pairing it with automated image analysis, this work provides a scalable, reproducible framework for screening natural product extracts for antiangiogenic activity — applicable beyond *C. infortunatum* to broader ethnopharmacological libraries.
